## The problem

Maritime and dry cargo brokerage still runs on email and Excel. A broker's day is spent opening
dozens of quote emails one by one, pulling the vessel or cargo details out by hand, comparing them
against separate spreadsheets, and trying to find the match. Ships, cargos, and the connections
between them get buried in threads.

It is slow, and on a busy day it is the kind of slow that loses deals. The information exists.
Getting an answer out of it takes an afternoon.

## What Celixion does

Celixion inverts the process. The moment a quote email arrives, the system reads it, files the
vessel or cargo record, and puts the most likely match in front of the broker — nobody moves between
an inbox and a spreadsheet any more.

Everything runs from one control centre:

- **Live fleet tracking** — vessels followed on a map in real time.
- **Smart matching** — incoming cargo and vessel listings compared automatically, with the best
  candidates surfaced first.
- **Market intelligence** — port congestion, market movement, and alerts on a single screen.
- **Operations tracking** — open work, quotes, and historical records managed in one place.

It does not replace the broker's judgement. It removes the twenty minutes of searching that used to
come before it.

## How it works

**Parsing pipelines.** Raw emails are ingested and broken down into candidate fields — vessel names,
dates, tonnage, ports, cargo types. Because the input is genuinely messy, parsing is treated as a
pipeline with stages rather than a single extraction step.

**AI-supported classification.** Language models handle the part that resists rigid rules: deciding
what a message actually is, and mapping loose phrasing onto the data model. The prompts live in the
codebase and are iterated on like any other component.

**A Supabase-backed data model.** Ships, cargos, and matches live in PostgreSQL behind Supabase,
with Prisma over the top. This is the layer everything else reads from — the entire point of the
system is that this data is finally structured enough to query.

**Automation and edge work.** n8n orchestrates the background workflows that read mail, resolve
ports and routes, and drive matching. Python covers data processing, and Cloudflare Workers sit at
the edge.

## Impact

The change shows up in the daily loop. What used to be constant inbox scanning and manual
cross-referencing between tables is now a match presented the moment the mail lands.

Since the mail pipeline went live at the end of April 2026 it has processed 23,453 emails — roughly
185 a day — into 2,450 vessel records and 5,339 cargo offers, with 92% accuracy on automatic
matching.

## On the stack

| Layer | Technology |
| --- | --- |
| Interface | Next.js, React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, shadcn/ui, Magic UI |
| Data | Supabase (Postgres + Auth), Prisma |
| Maps and tracking | Mapbox / Leaflet — live fleet visualisation |
| Automation | n8n, Python, Cloudflare Workers — mail ingestion, port and route resolution, matching |
| Reporting | Recharts — market and performance charts |
| Notifications | Automated email delivery and quote follow-up |

The stack was chosen for speed of iteration more than novelty. Every piece is something I can change
quickly while the product is still finding its shape — which, for a system whose hardest problem is
the unpredictability of its input, matters more than picking the most interesting tool.

## Who it's for

Maritime brokers and cargo companies — teams that have to track a large number of vessel and cargo
offers every day.
