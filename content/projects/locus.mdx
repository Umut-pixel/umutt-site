## The problem

Peritas Pet Food is a pet shop and veterinary distributor operating in the Aegean region of Turkey. All of their
customer, shipment, and receivables data lives in the Panorama (Univera) ERP — but it wasn't usable
by the people who actually needed it. To see how customers were distributed geographically, or to
find the ones whose deliveries had stalled or whose debt was piling up, someone had to export to
Excel and rebuild pivot tables and VLOOKUPs by hand, every single time.

The ERP's own reporting layer could not answer the questions the field team asked daily: *which
customers haven't had a delivery in 90 days?* *Who has an overdue balance past 56 days, and how
much?* The data existed. Getting an answer out of it took an afternoon.

## What Locus does

Locus ingests five different Panorama report exports — `MüşteriListesi` (customers),
`RutTanımListesi` (route definitions), `SevkiyatRaporuKüp` (shipment cube), `StYaşlandırma`
(receivables ageing) and `BelgeDetayRaporu` (document detail) — then cleans, joins, and renders them
as a single map coloured by risk.

It does not replace Panorama. It sits on top of it as a decision-support layer the field team uses
day to day.

- **The map.** 1,426 customers in the database, 1,313 of them placed on the map — clustered by
  location, coloured by risk state, with Mapbox Directions API integration for route verification.
  Neither risk axis is collapsed into the other.
- **Two separate risk axes.** *Delivery risk* is derived from time elapsed since the last shipment
  (healthy / watch / at-risk / never delivered). *Payment risk* comes from the receivables ageing
  bands — overdue amounts past 56 days, currently flagging 120 customers. These are deliberately kept
  apart rather than collapsed into one score, because they mean different things and need different
  responses.
- **Reporting.** An exportable customer table broken down by segment, risk, revenue, and open
  balance.
- **Data intake.** Manual Excel export from Panorama, an in-app upload flow, then parse and upsert
  into Supabase — plus a partial browser-automation sync layer built with n8n.

## Engineering decisions

**Risk is calculated in the database, in exactly one place.** All the risk logic lives in a single
SQL view (`musteriler_harita`). The frontend never re-implements or recomputes any of it. That makes
an entire class of bug — the number on screen disagreeing with the real number — structurally
impossible rather than merely unlikely.

**Confronting what the source data actually looked like.** The ERP export was not trustworthy on its
own. Revenue figures mixed VAT-inclusive and VAT-exclusive values. Column names lied — `KoordinatX`
was actually latitude. The district field was only 77% populated. Vehicle and plate fields held
placeholder constants. Each of these was found, documented, and handled in the ETL and view layer,
rather than papered over to produce false precision in the interface.

**Geocoding was deliberately kept shallow.** Street-level geocoding services (Nominatim) turned out
to be effectively 0% accurate for Turkish addresses in practice. So Locus uses neighbourhood-centroid
precision plus real GPS coordinates collected from the field. Where district information is missing,
nothing is guessed — the value is left empty. A wrong pin is worse than no pin.

**The ETL was built source-agnostic.** The client is moving to a different ERP and database provider
by the end of 2026, so the ingestion layer was designed from the start not to be coupled to
Panorama's specifics.

## Where it stands

First data load landed on 26 July 2026 and the automated Panorama sync has been running since
2 August; the system has been in continuous use ever since. Locus runs live on Vercel and Supabase
against real production data — 1,426 customers with real
revenue and receivables figures, not mock or demo data. The distribution above is the single most
useful thing the map surfaced: 63% of the customer base had no shipment on record at all, which is
invisible in the ERP and obvious the moment it is plotted.

The field and sales teams no longer pull exports from Panorama and process them in Excel. They
prioritise from a single map by delivery and payment risk, verify routes, and pull segment- and
risk-segmented reports from the same interface. The project is in active use by the Peritas team as an internal, single-tenant operations tool.
